Article 1

Who we are and what this notice covers

Fair Deal Properties Ltd ("Fair Deal Properties", "we", "us") is a company registered in the Federal Republic of Nigeria with its office at No. 4 Ekeh A. Yesufu Close, Utako, Abuja, Federal Capital Territory. We are a technology-enabled real estate agency and transaction facilitation firm.

We act as a data controller in respect of the personal data described in this notice. Where we engage service providers who process personal data on our instruction, they act as data processors and are bound by a written data processing agreement.

This notice applies to everyone whose personal data we process: visitors to this website, people who make enquiries, clients, prospective clients, members of cooperative societies we work with, sub-agents, developers, professional panel members and suppliers.

It is written in plain language. The General Application and Implementation Directive 2025 requires that information given to data subjects be clear, taking into account vulnerable individuals. If any part of this notice is unclear to you, contact our Data Protection Officer and we will explain it.

Article 2

The law we operate under

Our processing is governed by:

  • The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (NDPA), the principal statute.
  • The Nigeria Data Protection Act General Application and Implementation Directive 2025 (GAID), issued by the Nigeria Data Protection Commission on 20 March 2025 and effective from 19 September 2025, which replaced the Nigeria Data Protection Regulation 2019 and its Implementation Framework.
  • The Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022, which imposes customer due diligence and record-keeping obligations on real estate businesses as designated non-financial businesses and professions.
  • Other Nigerian legislation imposing record-keeping or disclosure obligations on us, including tax legislation.
Article 3

Personal data we collect

We collect only what we need for the purposes set out at Article 4. The categories are:

  • Identity data - full name, former names, date of birth, nationality, country of residence, photograph, and government identification numbers including Bank Verification Number, National Identification Number and passport or licence particulars where required for customer due diligence.
  • Contact data - residential and correspondence address, email address, telephone and WhatsApp number.
  • Financial data - income particulars, employment or business details, bank statements, existing obligations, source of funds declarations, equity contribution evidence, and tax particulars, where you engage us for mortgage facilitation or where customer due diligence requires it.
  • Transaction data - property preferences, budget, timeline, intended use, offers made, properties viewed, documents held on your behalf, and the record of your engagement with us.
  • Due diligence data - the outcome of politically exposed person screening, risk rating, and any enhanced due diligence we conduct.
  • Technical data - internet protocol address, browser type and version, device identifiers, pages visited, time spent and referral source, collected through this website.
  • Communications data - correspondence, consultation notes, and records of calls and messages between you and your named advisor.

Sensitive personal data. We do not seek sensitive personal data and do not require it for our services. Where any sensitive personal data is disclosed to us incidentally, we process it only where a lawful basis under the NDPA applies, and we will tell you which.

Children. The General Application and Implementation Directive 2025 defines a child as a person under 18 years of age. Our services are directed exclusively to adults. We do not knowingly collect personal data from a child. Where we become aware that we hold such data without the consent of a parent or guardian, we delete it.

Article 6

Who we share your data with

We do not sell personal data and we do not share it for any third party’s own marketing. We share only as follows, and only to the extent necessary:

  • Legal practitioners we instruct on your behalf for title investigation and conveyancing.
  • Registered Estate Surveyors and Valuers we instruct where a formal valuation is required.
  • Financing institutions - Primary Mortgage Banks, the Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria, participating commercial banks and other lenders - where you instruct us to submit an application, and only with your consent and only what the institution requires.
  • Licensed escrow providers, where an escrow arrangement is used.
  • Regulatory and law enforcement authorities - including the Nigeria Data Protection Commission, the Special Control Unit against Money Laundering, the Nigeria Financial Intelligence Unit and the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission - where required by law.
  • Service providers who process on our instruction under a written data processing agreement, including secure hosting, document storage, communications and accounting providers.
  • Our professional advisers, including auditors and insurers, under obligations of confidence.

Where we are required to make a report under the Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022, we are prohibited by that Act from notifying you of the report.

Article 7

Transfers of your data outside Nigeria

Because we serve diaspora clients and use cloud infrastructure, your personal data may be transferred to or accessed from outside Nigeria, including the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada, the European Union and the United Arab Emirates.

Under the NDPA and the General Application and Implementation Directive 2025, a transfer outside Nigeria requires an adequate safeguard or an applicable lawful basis. Where we transfer your data we rely on one of the following, and we will tell you which applies to you on request:

  • A determination by the Nigeria Data Protection Commission that the destination country provides an adequate level of protection.
  • Contractual safeguards binding the recipient to standards equivalent to those required under the NDPA.
  • Your explicit consent, given after we have informed you of the possible risks of the transfer in the absence of an adequacy determination or other safeguard.
  • Necessity for the performance of a contract with you, or for steps taken at your request before entering a contract.

We do not transfer personal data to any recipient that will not accept these obligations.

Cross-border transfer

Where your data is held, and why it leaves Nigeria

Your personal data is stored on infrastructure operated by Amazon Web Services in the Republic of Ireland (eu-west-1). Amazon Web Services operates no data centre in Nigeria, so storing your data with it necessarily transfers that data outside Nigeria.

We make that transfer on the basis in section 41(1)(a) of the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023: the recipient is bound by a law which provides an adequate level of protection. That law is Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the General Data Protection Regulation, which is in force in Ireland and which binds Amazon Web Services in respect of data it holds there. We rely in addition on the contractual clauses in our data processing addendum with Amazon Web Services, which is an independent basis under the same section.

We have recorded the legal basis for this transfer and our assessment of the adequacy of protection, as section 41(2) requires. We have also carried out a data protection impact assessment for this transfer, which Article 28(3)(o) of the General Application and Implementation Directive 2025 makes mandatory. Both are available to you, and to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission, on request.

We have not asserted, and do not rely on, any adequacy decision issued by the Commission in respect of Ireland. Whether the Commission has issued one is a matter for the Commission.

If you would prefer that your data not be transferred, tell us before you submit anything. We can take your instructions by telephone or in person and hold the record outside this website.

Article 9

Your rights

Under the NDPA you have the following rights. We do not charge for exercising them. The Act requires us to act without undue delay and does not fix a number of days; we commit to responding within 30 days as a matter of our own standard. Where a request is complex we may extend that period, and we will tell you why before the original period expires.

  • Right to be informed - to know what we do with your data. This notice is how we discharge it.
  • Right of access - to obtain a copy of the personal data we hold about you and information about how we use it.
  • Right to rectification - to have inaccurate or incomplete data corrected or completed without undue delay.
  • Right to erasure - to have your data deleted where it is no longer necessary, where you withdraw consent and no other basis applies, or where it has been unlawfully processed. This right does not override our legal retention obligations.
  • Right to restriction - to limit our processing while a dispute about accuracy or lawfulness is resolved.
  • Right to data portability - to receive data you provided to us in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format, and to have it transmitted to another controller where technically feasible.
  • Right to object - to object to processing based on legitimate interest, and to object to direct marketing at any time, with no exception.
  • Right to withdraw consent - at any time, as easily as it was given.
  • Rights in relation to automated decision-making - not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing which produces legal or similarly significant effects. We do not make such decisions. Our opportunity matching produces suggestions for a human advisor; it does not decide anything about you.
  • Right to lodge a complaint - with us, and with the Nigeria Data Protection Commission.
Article 10

How to exercise your rights

Use the Data Subject Rights Request page on this site, or write to our Data Protection Officer at the address below.

We commit to acknowledging within 5 business days and responding fully within 30 days. These are our own published commitments: section 34 of the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 requires us to act without constraint or unreasonable delay but sets no number of days, and we would rather tell you what we will actually do than quote you a deadline the Act does not contain. We may ask you to verify your identity before we act, to ensure we do not disclose your data to someone else.

If you are dissatisfied with our response. Article 40(2) and (3) of the General Application and Implementation Directive 2025 allow you to serve a Standard Notice to Address Grievance on us, in the form at Schedule 9 to that Directive. You may use that route with us. Serving it is not a precondition to complaining to the Commission - you may do either, or both. If the matter remains unresolved, you may escalate it to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission or institute civil proceedings. We will not require you to exhaust our internal process before approaching the Commission.

Article 11

How we protect your data

We apply technical and organizational measures proportionate to the risk, including:

  • Encryption of personal data at rest and in transit.
  • Role-based access control, with verification, advisory and finance functions holding separate permissions.
  • An append-only audit log recording who accessed which document, when and for what purpose. Clients may request their own access log.
  • Segregation of duties, so that no single person can originate, verify and approve the same transaction.
  • Confidentiality obligations and data protection training for every member of staff and every sub-agent.
  • Written data processing agreements with every supplier that processes personal data on our behalf.
  • Periodic review of our security posture and of this notice.

No transmission over the internet is entirely secure. We apply all reasonable measures, but we cannot guarantee absolute security, and we ask you to take care when sending personal information electronically.

Article 12

Data protection impact assessments

Where a processing activity is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of data subjects - in particular where it involves new technology, large-scale processing or sensitive personal data - we conduct a Data Protection Impact Assessment before the processing begins, document it, and make it available to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission on request.

Article 13

Personal data breaches

We maintain a breach response procedure. Where a personal data breach occurs, we:

  • Record it in our incident register immediately on becoming aware of it.
  • Assess the risk to affected data subjects without delay.
  • Notify the Nigeria Data Protection Commission within 72 hours of becoming aware, where the breach is notifiable, using the content required by the General Application and Implementation Directive 2025.
  • Notify affected data subjects without undue delay where the breach is likely to result in a high risk to their rights and freedoms, in clear language, describing what happened, what we are doing, and what they should do.
Article 14

Records of processing and compliance reporting

We maintain a record of processing activities covering each processing purpose, the categories of data and data subjects, the lawful basis relied on, recipients, international transfers and safeguards, retention periods and security measures. It is kept current and is available to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission on request.

Where we are designated a Data Controller or Data Processor of Major Importance under Article 9 of the General Application and Implementation Directive 2025, we will register with the Commission, conduct the required data protection audit and file Compliance Audit Returns within the prescribed timelines. Our Data Protection Officer prepares semi-annual internal data protection reports for management.

Our designation status and registration particulars are reviewed against the current thresholds and will be published here once determined.

Article 15

Changes to this notice

We review this notice at least annually and whenever our processing changes materially. The version number and effective date at the top show the current version. Material changes are notified on this website and, where the change affects an existing engagement, directly to the client.

We retain superseded versions and will provide any earlier version on request, so that you can see the terms that applied at the time of your engagement.

Supervisory authority. The Nigeria Data Protection Commission supervises compliance with the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023. You have the right to lodge a complaint with the Commission at any time. We would ask you to raise it with our Data Protection Officer first so that we have the opportunity to put it right, but you are under no obligation to do so.
